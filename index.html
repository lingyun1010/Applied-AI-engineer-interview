01 / ROLE

Read the role correctly

This is a permanent internal builder role organised into three-to-six-month business rotations. The rotation changes; the employment does not.

Nuix in one sentence

“Finding truth in a digital world”

Nuix builds software that helps organisations turn complex data into actionable intelligence. The role sits inside its new internal AI capability, intended to connect AI activity with measurable business impact.

中文提示

不要把 Nuix 只说成“做 AI 的公司”。它的核心是复杂数据、调查/法律科技与可信结果；AI Builder 是内部转型能力。

The operating model

Embedded builder, not slideware consultant

You embed in a function, discover the workflow, time-box a POC, decide build/pause/abandon, ship into the live environment, instrument it, learn, and leave reusable knowledge for the next rotation.

中文提示

重点不是“会用 LLM”，而是能把模糊业务问题变成可上线、可评估、可运维的系统。

Discover

Map current workflows, stakeholders, risk, value and clear exit criteria.

Build

RAG, document processing, agentic workflows, MCP/API integrations and reusable skill files.

Operate

Evaluation, observability, production debugging, latency/cost judgement and iteration.

Multiply

Communicate with leaders, support AI advocates, and document patterns for the wider cohort.

Ambiguity

Can you impose structure without waiting for a perfect brief?

Ownership

Can you own discovery through monitoring—not hand off the hard parts?

Judgement

Can you stop an attractive POC when evidence says it will not pay back?

Influence

Can you be credible with both functional leaders and engineers?

02 / INTRO

Your opening

Use the shorter version first. Expand only when the interviewer gives you room.

60-second version
“I’m an experienced software and data engineer who has been moving deliberately into applied AI. I have a PhD in computer vision and more than seven years of engineering experience across Java, JavaScript, React, APIs, AWS, Docker, PostgreSQL and data platforms. More recently, I’ve been building GenAI systems that combine retrieval, structured knowledge, routing, tool use and human review—through EvidMatch, an e-commerce RAG support prototype and the Love At Home platform. What attracts me to this role is that it is not an advisory AI position. It starts with an ambiguous operational problem and expects one person to work with stakeholders, structure the use case, build the solution and stay accountable after launch. That combination of hands-on delivery, business process and enterprise impact is exactly where I want to take my next step.”
90-second version
“My background combines research depth with practical software delivery. I completed a PhD in computer vision at Queen Mary University of London, then spent more than seven years in software and big-data engineering, working across backend services, frontend products, APIs, AWS, Docker, PostgreSQL, Solr, testing and deployment. That gave me strong production habits: clarify the problem, design for failure, make trade-offs explicit and work closely with the people who use the system.

Over the last phase of my career, I’ve focused that engineering experience on applied GenAI. EvidMatch explores how structured project evidence can become trustworthy, reviewable outputs; my e-commerce RAG prototype combines structured product and policy knowledge with intent routing and escalation; and Love At Home is a real service-business platform where I have had to connect product decisions with operational workflows, reliability and privacy.

I’m honest that my enterprise AI production depth is still developing. I’m not presenting prototypes as years of large-scale production AI. What I do bring is mature engineering judgement, direct stakeholder experience and evidence that I can learn and build quickly. Nuix is compelling because the role turns those strengths into full-lifecycle AI delivery inside complex functions, with a team, governance and real organisational impact.”
中文提示

开场顺序：成熟工程师 → 应用 AI 转型 → 三个证据 → 为什么这个岗位。不要从“我缺少什么”开始；差距放在最后主动、简短、可控地说明。

04 / MOTIVATION

Why an established company and team?

Your strongest answer is not “my startup did not work”. It is “building independently clarified what scale of problems and collaboration I want next”.

Collaboration

Sharper decisions

Independent building strengthened ownership. Now you want peers who challenge architecture, share domain context and improve the quality of decisions.

Complexity

Enterprise constraints

You want to work with real internal systems, governance, sensitive data, multiple stakeholders and operational consequences that cannot be simulated in a solo project.

Impact

Longer compounding loop

You want to stay with solutions through adoption, monitoring and iteration, while reusable patterns compound across teams.

Core answer · the version to practise hardest
“Building my own products has been valuable because it forced me to own every decision—from understanding the user problem to architecture, delivery and feedback. It also clarified what I want next. I do not want to build only in isolation. The problems that interest me most now are the ones that require strong collaboration: deep domain knowledge, experienced technical peers, security and governance partners, and business leaders who own the outcome.

An established company gives me access to complex enterprise workflows and the responsibility to make AI work within real constraints, not around them. A team also creates a better quality bar: ideas are challenged, patterns are reused and lessons can compound across projects. What appeals to me about Nuix specifically is that I would keep the ownership and speed I developed as an independent builder, but apply them with a capable team to problems that have a longer horizon and broader organisational impact.”
中文提示

语气要是“主动选择更大的协作和复杂度”，不是“创业失败所以找稳定工作”。不要贬低自己的项目；它们证明 ownership，而 Nuix 是下一阶段的放大器。

05 / EVIDENCE

Three truthful project stories

Each story needs a business problem, your decision, what you built, and the boundary between current proof and production maturity.

01

EvidMatch

Evidence-led AI product
Problem
People have useful project evidence, but it is fragmented and difficult to turn into trustworthy, context-specific proof.
Approach
Structure projects, milestones and evidence so AI-assisted outputs can remain grounded in source material and under explicit user control.
Your angle
Product architecture, APIs, data and permission boundaries, applied AI workflow design and a clear human-in-the-loop publication boundary.
Truth boundary
Describe the features you personally built and verified. Do not imply enterprise-wide adoption or production scale.

Use for: ambiguous product definition, trust, tool integration, data boundaries and end-to-end ownership.

02

E-commerce RAG prototype

RAG · routing · escalation
Problem
Customer questions span products, policies and FAQs; not every request should be answered by retrieval alone.
Approach
Create structured knowledge, route intent, retrieve the relevant context, constrain the response and escalate when confidence or policy requires it.
Your angle
RAG design, structured knowledge generation, tool-like routing and human escalation thinking.
Truth boundary
Call it a prototype. Discuss the evaluation and observability you would require before real customer traffic.

Use for: RAG, POC discipline, build/pause criteria, failure modes and responsible automation.

03

Love At Home

Operational full-stack delivery
Problem
A service business needs a trustworthy path from public enquiry to operational follow-up.
Approach
Build the website and enquiry workflow with validation, persistence, email/Sheets integrations, idempotency and deployment considerations.
Your angle
Translate non-technical needs into a working system, connect multiple services and diagnose failures across application and deployment boundaries.
Truth boundary
This is strongest as proof of engineering and stakeholder ownership—not as an agentic AI case study.

Use for: client consulting, workflow mapping, reliability, privacy, integrations and production-minded delivery.

中文提示

Charlie 追问时不要一次讲完所有技术。先讲业务流程和关键决策，再根据问题进入 RAG、权限、部署或失败处理。每个项目准备一个真实的“我改变了什么决定”。

06 / GAPS

Be honest without underselling

Name the gap precisely, show the adjacent evidence, and explain the concrete operating discipline you would use.

Python as the primary language

You have Python exposure through AI/computer-vision work, but your deepest production engineering history is broader: Java, JavaScript and data systems.

Say: “I would not claim the same depth in production Python as in my established stack. The underlying engineering practices transfer, and I’m deliberately using Python more deeply in my applied AI work.”

Enterprise production AI

You have credible prototypes and production software experience, but not years of enterprise GenAI systems operating at Nuix scale.

Say: “My honest gap is scale and duration in enterprise AI production—not ownership or production engineering. I would rather be precise about that and show how I reduce the risk.”

Evaluation & observability

You understand the need, but should not imply a mature, long-running AI evaluation platform if you have not operated one.

Say: “Before launch I would define task-specific evals, failure categories, a baseline, human review and operational telemetry; after launch I would monitor quality, latency, cost and business outcomes.”
If challenged on the production requirement
“That is a fair challenge. I would separate two things. I already have mature software-engineering experience: shipping systems, integrating services, debugging across environments and working directly with stakeholders. My enterprise GenAI production experience is newer, and I would not overstate it. The reason I believe I can make the step is that the missing part is specific operating depth in a new technical layer—not the fundamentals of ownership, system design or production delivery. I would approach the role with explicit evals, staged releases, observability and clear escalation rather than treating an impressive demo as production-ready.”

08 / ANSWERS

Likely interview questions

Open each question to rehearse. Keep recruiter answers concise; let Charlie pull you deeper technically.

Walk me through how you consult with a stakeholder on an unclear AI opportunity.

“I begin with the workflow, not the model. I identify the user, trigger, current steps, hand-offs, pain, risk and what a better outcome would change. Then I separate automation from decision support, check data access and failure consequences, and define a small test with exit criteria. I play back the process in plain language before building. The outcome can be build, pause or abandon; a fast ‘no’ is useful if it is evidence-based.”

Proof to attach: Love At Home for translating operational needs; e-commerce RAG for automation versus escalation.

Tell me about a time you worked directly with a client or non-technical stakeholder.

Use one real stakeholder from Love At Home. Explain what they needed in business language, where the initial request was ambiguous, how you confirmed the workflow, what trade-off you surfaced and how you demonstrated the result. Keep personal/client information private.

Structure: context → misunderstanding/risk → your translation → shipped result → what you learnt.

How do you decide whether a POC should become a production build?

“I agree the decision criteria before the POC: user value, quality threshold, data availability, operational risk, integration effort, latency and cost. I test against representative cases, including failure cases. I recommend build only when the evidence supports both usefulness and operability. Otherwise I narrow the scope, pause for a missing dependency or abandon it—and document why.”

How would you evaluate a RAG or agentic workflow?

“I would split the system into stages: routing, retrieval, generation or tool selection, and the final business outcome. For each stage I would define a labelled test set and failure categories. I would measure retrieval relevance, groundedness, task completion, unsafe or unauthorised actions, latency and cost. High-risk actions need independent authorisation and human review. Production monitoring then checks drift and real failure patterns against the pre-launch baseline.”

What does “Principal” mean if you are an individual contributor?

“To me it means leverage and judgement, not just individual output. I should handle ambiguity with limited supervision, make sound build-versus-stop decisions, communicate with senior stakeholders, raise the engineering quality bar and leave reusable patterns that make other builders faster.”

Why are you leaving your own projects for a company role?

Use the established-company answer above. Emphasise that independent work proved ownership and clarified your appetite for collaboration, enterprise complexity and longer-term impact. Never frame the projects as wasted effort.

Are you comfortable with three days per week in the Sydney office?

“Yes. I am Sydney-based and comfortable with the three-day office expectation. For an embedded role, being present with the function and technical team is valuable for discovery, trust and rapid feedback.”

What are your salary expectations, notice period and work rights?

Do not memorise invented facts. State your exact work-rights and notice-period position. For salary: “I’m most interested in the role, scope and overall package. I’d be happy to understand the approved range and then confirm whether we are aligned.” If pressed, give the real range you have decided in advance.

09 / QUESTIONS

Thoughtful questions

Use two with Becky; save technical operating-model questions for Charlie.

For Becky

Process & success

  • “How is the interview process structured after the meeting with Charlie?”

Operating model
